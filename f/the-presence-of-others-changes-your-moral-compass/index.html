The presence of others changes your moral compass.

The bystander effect demonstrates that your likelihood of intervening in an emergency decreases as the number of witnesses increases.

In a 1968 experiment, participants were significantly slower to seek help when they believed others were also watching the event. Diffusion of responsibility makes inaction feel like a shared, and therefore acceptable, decision.

The larger the crowd, the more invisible your responsibility.
