You stop helping the moment a witness appears.

The bystander effect shows that the presence of other people decreases your likelihood of acting in an emergency.

When multiple people witness an event, individual responsibility is diffused. You wait for someone else to act first, and the group remains frozen, assuming someone more qualified will intervene.

The more witnesses there are, the less likely anyone is to move.

Latané and Darley, 1968
