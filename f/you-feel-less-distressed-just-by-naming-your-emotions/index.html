You feel less distressed just by naming your emotions.

Affect labeling—putting your feelings into words—significantly reduces activity in the amygdala, the brain's emotional center.

When you identify a negative emotion like 'anxiety' or 'frustration,' you engage the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex. This shifts your brain's processing from a raw emotional response to a more cognitive analysis. It acts as a regulatory brake on your immediate reaction.

You cannot easily feel and analyze the same emotion at once.
