You unconsciously copy the sentence structure of people you speak to.

Syntactic priming makes you more likely to repeat the grammatical structure of the sentence you just heard.

If your conversation partner uses a passive voice, you are statistically more likely to use a passive voice in your next sentence. This is not a conscious mimicry of style, but an automatic adaptation of your internal language processing. This alignment reduces the cognitive load required to understand one another.

Your speech patterns are a reflection of the person across from you.
