You hold losing investments far longer than winning ones.

A 1985 study found that investors are significantly more likely to sell a stock that has gained value than one that has dropped.

We assign psychological pain to the realization of a loss. By holding onto a losing asset, we postpone that pain and hold onto the hope of a break-even point. This prevents us from allocating capital rationally.

You are wired to avoid the emotional hit of admitting you were wrong.
