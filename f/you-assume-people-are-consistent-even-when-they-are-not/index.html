You assume people are consistent even when they are not.

The 'Correspondence Bias' leads you to attribute behavior to personality rather than the situation.

A 1967 study found that even when participants knew a speaker was assigned a specific viewpoint, they still rated the speaker as genuinely believing that view. You are hardwired to look for internal traits in others. You ignore the situational pressures that are plainly visible to you.

You think people are their actions, but they are just reacting to the moment.

Where this comes from

Study
Jones and Harris (1967) on attribution
What the check found
The study (Jones & Harris, 1967) is a foundational experiment in social psychology that accurately demonstrates the correspondence bias, though 'hardwired' is an overstatement of a cognitive tendency.
