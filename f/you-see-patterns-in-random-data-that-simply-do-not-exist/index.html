patterns

You see patterns in random data that simply do not exist.

The 1985 Gilovich, Vallone, and Tversky study focused on basketball shooting performance, but utilized the known psychological tendency to misperceive random sequences (like those in coin flips) to explain why fans and players mistakenly perceive a 'hot hand'.

The study analyzed basketball shots to illustrate the 'clustering illusion' rather than conducting an empirical coin-flipping test.

You often interpret noise as a signal just to make sense of the world.

Where this comes from

Study
Gilovich, Vallone, and Tversky (1985), Cognitive Psychology
What the check found
The 1985 study is the correct source for the theory, but the claim misrepresents the primary empirical subject of the paper, which was basketball performance, not a coin-flipping experiment.

Solid, with limits
