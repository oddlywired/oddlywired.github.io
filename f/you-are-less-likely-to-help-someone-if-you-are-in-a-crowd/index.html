You are less likely to help someone if you are in a crowd.

The bystander effect demonstrates that the presence of others reduces your perceived personal responsibility to intervene.

A 1968 study found that individuals were much slower to report an emergency—such as smoke filling a room—when others were present compared to when they were alone. You look to others to define the situation; when they remain passive, you interpret the silence as confirmation that no action is required. You wait for someone else to act first.

In a group, your conscience is deferred to the inaction of strangers.

Where this comes from

Study
Darley and Latané, 1968, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
The core premise and attribution are correct, though the effect is nuanced by later research showing it does not manifest in high-danger scenarios.
