judgment

You judge an explanation as 'correct' if it includes neuroscience data.

The seductive allure of neuroscience makes people believe that explanations are logically sound, even when they contain completely irrelevant brain science.

Including mentions of brain regions or neural activity in a weak argument causes people to view that argument as much more credible. Your brain values the authoritative tone of scientific terminology, even when it adds no actual explanatory power. You are susceptible to the aesthetic of science rather than the rigor.

The mention of the brain can trick you into accepting an error.

Weisberg et al., 2008, Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience
