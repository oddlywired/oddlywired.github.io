A random number changes how you value a price.

A 1974 study found that people estimated the percentage of African nations in the UN differently depending on a random number spun on a wheel.

Even though the number was clearly random and irrelevant, participants who spun a higher number provided significantly higher estimates. Your brain uses the first available anchor to build its entire scale of judgment.

Your final decision is often just an adjustment of the first number you saw.

Where this comes from

Study
Tversky and Kahneman (1974), Anchoring Effect
