You confuse high physiological arousal for romantic attraction.

The brain often misinterprets physical adrenaline or fear as feelings of attraction toward a person nearby.

In a 1974 study, men crossing a high, swaying bridge were more likely to call a female interviewer than those who crossed a low, stable bridge. Your brain looks for a reason for your racing heart and finds a person.

You often mistake tension for chemistry.

Where this comes from

Study
Dutton and Aron, 1974
What the check found
Dutton & Aron (1974) is a seminal study on misattribution of arousal. While the finding is robust in the literature, 'often' overstates the frequency of this phenomenon in general life.
