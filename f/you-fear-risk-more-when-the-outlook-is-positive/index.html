You fear risk more when the outlook is positive.

People show a strong preference for certainty when a positive outcome is likely, even when a riskier option offers a higher expected value.

The pseudocertainty effect explains why individuals choose a sure gain over a gamble, even if the math favors the gamble. It demonstrates how emotional framing shifts risk tolerance regardless of the statistical reality.

A sure thing feels safer than a better thing.

Where this comes from

Study
Tversky and Kahneman, 1981
What the check found
The attribution is correct (Kahneman & Tversky, 1981, 'The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice'). The phenomenon describes how reducing risk to zero (certainty) is preferred over a higher mathematical expectation.
