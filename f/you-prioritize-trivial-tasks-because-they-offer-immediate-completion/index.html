mere urgency

You prioritize trivial tasks because they offer immediate completion.

The mere urgency effect shows people choose tasks with shorter deadlines over tasks with higher rewards.

Participants were offered a high-value task with a long deadline and a low-value task with an immediate one. Despite the rational choice being the high-value task, subjects repeatedly chose the shorter deadline task. The feeling of finishing a task acts as a psychological reward that overrides the long-term utility.

You are often working toward closure rather than true progress.
