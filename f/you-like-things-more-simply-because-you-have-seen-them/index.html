You like things more simply because you have seen them.

The mere exposure effect is the tendency to develop a preference for stimuli merely because you are familiar with them.

Participants showed increased liking for abstract shapes or nonsense words after being exposed to them repeatedly, even when they could not consciously remember seeing those specific items before.

Your familiarity is often mistaken for quality.
