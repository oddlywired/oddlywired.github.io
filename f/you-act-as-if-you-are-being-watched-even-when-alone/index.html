You act as if you are being watched even when alone.

A 2006 study showed that people are significantly more honest in tasks when a picture of eyes is displayed nearby.

The 'watching-eyes' effect taps into your deep-seated social conditioning. Even a symbolic representation of a gaze triggers an unconscious awareness of reputation. You alter your behavior to align with social norms, even when you know no one is actually present.

Your behavior is fundamentally shaped by an imagined audience.

Where this comes from

Study
Bateson, Nettle, and Roberts (2006), Biology Letters
What the check found
The citation and study details are accurate. While the effect has faced scrutiny in recent years due to replication failures, the claim correctly reflects the findings of the 2006 paper.
