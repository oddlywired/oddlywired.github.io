performance

You perform worse when you try to consciously control movement.

Studies on 'choking under pressure' show that focusing on automatic movements impairs skill execution.

The conscious processing hypothesis suggests that pressure forces you to monitor well-learned motor skills. By attempting to consciously guide actions that have become procedural, you disrupt the brain's efficient execution loop. You move from fluid, rapid performance to jerky, error-prone execution.

Thinking about how you do something is often the best way to fail at it.
