decision making

You judge past decisions by their results, not their logic.

The outcome bias causes people to retrospectively evaluate a decision as poor if the result was unfavorable, even if the decision-making process was sound.

Researchers have found that when people evaluate scenarios with identical risks, they rate the decision-maker more harshly if the outcome was negative. This blinds you to the quality of the strategy employed at the time. You confuse bad luck with bad judgment.

A good process does not always guarantee a successful outcome.

Baron and Hershey, 1988, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
