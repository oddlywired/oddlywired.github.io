memory

Your memory stores only the gist of experiences.

Leveling and sharpening causes you to omit details while exaggerating specific core elements when recalling a story or event.

As time passes, your memory naturally simplifies complex accounts by removing minor details (leveling) and making prominent features more distinct (sharpening). You are not a camera recording reality, but an editor constantly rewriting your personal history to fit a coherent narrative. The resulting memory is a simplified construct, not an accurate reproduction.

You remember the meaning, not the event.
