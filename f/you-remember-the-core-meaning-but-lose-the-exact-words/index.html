You remember the core meaning but lose the exact words.

The verbatim effect states that you are much more likely to remember the meaning of a sentence than the specific words used to express it.

Your brain encodes information semantically, stripping away syntax and literal phrasing in favor of the gist. While you may feel you recall exactly what someone said, you are actually reconstructing the memory from the underlying concepts. The literal details are the first thing your brain discards to save space.

You store the summary, never the transcript.

Where this comes from

Study
Sachs, 1967, Perception & Psychophysics
What the check found
Jacqueline Sachs' 1967 study is the seminal work establishing that memory for linguistic meaning persists significantly longer than memory for exact syntax.
