Events do not make you as happy as expected.

The hedonic treadmill, a concept proposed by Brickman and Campbell in 1971, hypothesizes a tendency to return to a baseline happiness level, though modern psychological research suggests this adaptation is not universal.

The theory builds upon Helson's 1964 Adaptation-Level Theory, but the 1971 Brickman and Campbell paper is titled 'Hedonic Relativism and Planning the Good Society.'

Your baseline happiness is more resilient than you assume it is.
