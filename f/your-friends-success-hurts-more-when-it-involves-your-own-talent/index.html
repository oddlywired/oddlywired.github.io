Your friends' success hurts more when it involves your own talent.

The self-evaluation maintenance model suggests you feel threatened when a close friend outperforms you in a domain essential to your self-identity.

Research shows that if your friend succeeds in a field you do not care about, you bask in their glow, but if they excel in your niche, you perceive their success as a threat to your own value. Your self-esteem is balanced against your physical and emotional proximity to the other person.

Your support for friends is conditional on the areas where you define yourself.
