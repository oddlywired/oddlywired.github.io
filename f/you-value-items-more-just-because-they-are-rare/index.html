You value items more just because they are rare.

A 1975 study showed that participants rated cookies as more desirable when they were presented in a nearly empty jar.

When supply is restricted, your brain automatically increases the perceived value of the object to prevent the loss of potential access. The competition against others or the simple threat of unavailability overrides the objective utility of the item.

The item is not better, only harder for you to get.

Where this comes from

Study
Worchel, Lee, and Adewole, 1975
What the check found
The study confirmed that scarcity increases perceived value. The claim is accurate, though it describes only the static portion of a more nuanced finding involving supply transitions.
