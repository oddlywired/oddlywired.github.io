You judge harmful inaction as less severe than action.

Research indicates that people rate harmful consequences resulting from inaction as more acceptable than identical harm caused by active intervention.

This omission bias persists even when the final outcome of the inaction and the action are objectively the same. Participants consistently assign more moral blame to the person who intervened, even if the harm caused by doing nothing was equivalent.

We value doing nothing over taking responsibility for change.
