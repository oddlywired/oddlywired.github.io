You judge your actions by circumstances but others by character.

Research in the 1970s proposed an actor-observer asymmetry in attribution, though modern meta-analyses indicate this effect is often smaller, less consistent, or culturally dependent than early studies suggested.

The effect is not a universal psychological law but rather a phenomenon that varies significantly based on experimental design, cultural context, and the nature of the observed behavior.
