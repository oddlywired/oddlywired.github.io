You unconsciously mimic the body language of others.

People consistently adopt the postures and mannerisms of their conversation partners, a process termed the chameleon effect.

Researchers found that participants shifted their movements to match those of an accomplice during a social interaction. This mirroring happens automatically and increases the other person's affinity for you.

Your body creates social cohesion before you start speaking.

Where this comes from

Study
Chartrand and Bargh, 1999, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
The core of the claim is well-supported by the 1999 study. The word 'consistently' is a slight generalization of a psychological tendency, but it aligns with the study's established findings on the mimicry-affinity link.
