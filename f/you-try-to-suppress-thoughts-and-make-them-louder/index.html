You try to suppress thoughts and make them louder.

The 'ironic process theory' suggests that the effort to suppress a specific thought makes it more cognitively accessible.

When participants were instructed not to think of a white bear, the thought became a preoccupation that returned more frequently than in those who were not told to suppress it.

You cannot fight a thought by trying to banish it.
