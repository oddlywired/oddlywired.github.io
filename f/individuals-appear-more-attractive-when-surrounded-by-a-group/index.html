Individuals appear more attractive when surrounded by a group.

The cheerleader effect is a perceptual bias where a person is rated as slightly more attractive in a group than in isolation, as described by Walker and Vul in 2013.

The publication year was 2013, and the measured increase in attractiveness is 1.5–2.0%.

Your brain is mathematically smoothing out your individual traits.
