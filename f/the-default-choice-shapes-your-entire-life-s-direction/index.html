The default choice shapes your entire life's direction.

While researchers like Johnson and Goldstein have shown that default settings correlate with higher registration rates, the actual number of transplants is strongly influenced by healthcare infrastructure, public trust, and logistical coordination, rather than being solely driven by the opt-out default.

Opt-out policies do not guarantee higher donation rates; countries like Greece maintain low rates despite having an opt-out system, while successful nations like Spain attribute their high rates primarily to systemic infrastructure rather than their legislative default.

Default options are rarely as neutral as they appear.
