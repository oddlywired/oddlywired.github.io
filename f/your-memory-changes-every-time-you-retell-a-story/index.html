Your memory changes every time you retell a story.

A 1932 study demonstrated that details of a complex narrative disappear or distort after repeated recounting.

Participants were asked to recall a folk tale at increasing intervals. The stories became shorter, more conventional, and better suited to the participants' own cultural expectations over time.

Your version of the past is a reconstruction, not a recording.
