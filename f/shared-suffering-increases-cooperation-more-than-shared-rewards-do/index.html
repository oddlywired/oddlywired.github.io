cooperation

Shared suffering increases cooperation more than shared rewards do.

Groups that endure an unpleasant experience together develop stronger bonds than groups that experience a neutral activity together.

The research specifically compares painful experiences to non-painful (neutral) experiences rather than success-based experiences.

Cohesion is forged in the struggle, not the outcome.

Where this comes from

Study
Bastian et al., 2014, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin
What the check found
Bastian et al. (2014) confirms that shared pain increases affiliation, but does not claim it is stronger than the bonding achieved through shared success.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
