You can deliberately choose to forget useless information.

Directed forgetting shows that you can reduce the accessibility of specific information when given a signal to do so.

In 1970, studies showed that participants who were told a list of items was no longer needed performed worse on recall tasks than those told to keep the information. The brain appears to actively inhibit the retrieval of items labeled as irrelevant.

Your brain is an active editor of your own memory.
