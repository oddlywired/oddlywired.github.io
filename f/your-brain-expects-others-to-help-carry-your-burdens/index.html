Your brain expects others to help carry your burdens.

Social Baseline Theory suggests the human brain perceives the world as requiring less effort when a social partner is present.

A 2008 study found that when participants held the hand of a partner, they showed reduced threat-related neural activity compared to when they were alone. The brain treats social closeness as a shared resource, effectively lowering the perceived physical cost of navigating your environment. When you are alone, the task appears objectively more difficult to your nervous system.

You are physically more resilient when you believe help is within reach.

Where this comes from

Study
Coan et al. (2006) on social baseline theory
What the check found
The core of Social Baseline Theory is accurately described as the brain expecting social proximity as the baseline to minimize neural and metabolic effort, but the supporting text misidentifies the landmark publication date.
