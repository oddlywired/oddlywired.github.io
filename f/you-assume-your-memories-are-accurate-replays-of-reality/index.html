memory

You assume your memories are accurate replays of reality.

Human memory is a reconstructive process where, under certain conditions, a retrieved memory can enter a labile state and undergo reconsolidation, making it susceptible to modification.

Modification does not occur automatically every time a memory is recalled; it is a context-dependent process.

Every time you look back, you rewrite the past.

Where this comes from

Study
Nader and Hardt, 2009, Nature Reviews Neuroscience
What the check found
While reconsolidation theory (Nader and Hardt) is well-supported, the claim falsely implies that this process is automatic and universal with every retrieval, rather than conditional.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
