negativity bias

You weight negative information more heavily than positive feedback.

The negativity bias causes you to focus on threats and failures far more than gains.

Across various psychological contexts, bad events or traits have a greater impact on your mind than equivalent good ones. This is an adaptive mechanism for survival, as detecting a threat is often more critical than finding a reward. Even in stable environments, this tendency keeps your attention locked on potential risks.

Your mind scans for trouble long after the environment is safe.

Where this comes from

Study
Rozin & Royzman, 2001
What the check found
The cited paper by Rozin and Royzman (2001) is the definitive source for this concept, confirming that negative events hold more psychological weight, salience, and impact than positive equivalents.
