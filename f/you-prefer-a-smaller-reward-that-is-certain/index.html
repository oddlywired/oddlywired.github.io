decision making

You prefer a smaller reward that is certain.

Research shows people systematically choose lower-value, certain rewards over higher-value options involving uncertainty.

The mere presence of uncertainty triggers a disproportionate avoidance response. This happens even when the expected value of the uncertain option is mathematically higher. We view the 'unknown' as a cost that must be deducted from the reward.

Your aversion to the unknown costs you more than you realize.

Where this comes from

Study
Gneezy, List, & Wu, 2006, Quarterly Journal of Economics
What the check found
Gneezy, List, and Wu (2006) did publish this study in the QJE. While the effect is well-documented in behavioral economics, the magnitude is frequently debated and subject to replication variability.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
