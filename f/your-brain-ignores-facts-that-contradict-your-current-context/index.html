Your brain ignores facts that contradict your current context.

The Moses illusion occurs when people fail to notice factual errors in sentences because the question contains a semantically related entity.

In studies, people frequently answer 'two' when asked how many animals of each kind Moses took on the ark. The brain processes the sentence as a coherent narrative, prioritizing ease of understanding over verification of details.

You hear what you expect, not what is said.
