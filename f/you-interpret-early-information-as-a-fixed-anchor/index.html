decision making

You interpret early information as a fixed anchor.

The anchoring bias describes how the first number or piece of data you receive heavily influences all subsequent estimates.

If you are asked to estimate a quantity after being shown a large number, your answer will be higher than if you were primed with a small one, even when the number is irrelevant to the task. Your mind uses the first available value as a cognitive anchor, and you struggle to adjust your judgment far enough away from it.

Your final decision often starts with an arbitrary number.
