memory

You remember the message but forget the unreliable source.

The sleeper effect occurs when the persuasiveness of a message increases over time as you decouple it from its source.

Initially, you might discount a claim because you heard it from an unreliable source. Weeks later, you remember the information but lose the context of who said it, making the claim seem more credible.

Your memory often discards context while holding onto the content.

Hovland, Lumsdaine, and Sheffield (1949)
