The presence of others makes you less likely to act.

A 1968 study found that individuals are significantly less likely to intervene in an emergency when they believe others are observing the scene.

This is the bystander effect. Responsibility is diffused across the group, causing each person to wait for someone else to step forward first. You feel less pressure to act when you are surrounded by potential actors.

Your sense of responsibility shrinks in proportion to the number of witnesses.

Where this comes from

Study
Darley and Latané, 1968
What the check found
