You remember the intensity of an experience, not duration.

The peak-end rule explains how you evaluate past events based on their most intense moment and their ending.

In a 1993 study, participants chose to extend an uncomfortable experience because the final moments were slightly less painful than the start. The brain ignores the total time spent and creates a summary based on these two specific markers.

You are recording a movie, not keeping a ledger.

Where this comes from

Study
Daniel Kahneman, 1993
What the check found
The 1993 paper 'When more pain is preferred to less: adding a better end' by Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber, and Redelmeier is the definitive source for this finding.
