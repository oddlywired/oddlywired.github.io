anchoring

A random number changes what you pay today.

In a 1974 study, people asked if the percentage of African nations in the UN was higher or lower than 10% guessed lower than those asked if it was higher or lower than 65%.

This anchoring effect shows that the first piece of information you receive sets a mental baseline. Even when that number is clearly random or irrelevant, your subsequent estimates pull toward it like a magnet.

You are rarely starting from zero when you make an estimate.
