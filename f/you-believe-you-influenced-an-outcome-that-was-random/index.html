illusion of control

You believe you influenced an outcome that was random.

When individuals are given a choice in a game of chance, they rate their probability of success as higher than those who were assigned an outcome.

This is the illusion of control. By performing an action—like throwing dice or picking a card—you trick your brain into assuming a causal link between your effort and the random result. You struggle to accept that some systems are entirely outside your influence.

You treat chance as if it were a skill.
