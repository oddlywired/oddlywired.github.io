You overestimate your ability on tasks you find difficult.

The hard-easy effect describes the tendency to overestimate one's own performance on hard tasks and underestimate it on easy ones.

When a task is difficult, individuals fail to account for how likely they are to miss critical information. Conversely, when a task is easy, people assume they are 'above average' and underestimate the likelihood that others will perform equally well.

Confidence is a poor predictor of difficulty.

Where this comes from

Study
Lichtenstein and Fischhoff, 1977
What the check found
The definition of the hard-easy effect is accurate, and Lichtenstein and Fischhoff (1977) are the correct authors for this foundational research in calibration of probability judgments.
