You feel worse when your friend succeeds.

Self-evaluation maintenance theory explains why your self-esteem drops when a close friend performs exceptionally well.

When someone close to you excels in a field central to your identity, it creates a social comparison that threatens your self-view. You only feel happy for them if the domain is irrelevant to your own self-worth. You are essentially measuring yourself against their success.

Your joy for others depends on how you define yourself.

Where this comes from

Study
Tesser, 1988, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology
