self esteem

You believe you possess more talent than the average.

The better-than-average effect is a widespread cognitive bias where individuals rate their abilities as superior to their peers.

In a classic study, 88% of American drivers rated their driving skills as above the median. Because the median cannot logically be exceeded by 88% of a population, it confirms a pervasive inflation of self-assessment.

You measure your competence against a version of your peers you’ve minimized.

Where this comes from

Study
Ola Svenson, 1981, Acta Psychologica
What the check found
The figure and attribution are accurate to Svenson's 1981 research. The effect is widely replicated across various domains of ability, though the magnitude fluctuates depending on task difficulty.
