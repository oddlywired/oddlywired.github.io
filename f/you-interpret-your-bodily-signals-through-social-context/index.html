two factor theory

You interpret your bodily signals through social context.

Physiological arousal is ambiguous, and you use your environment to identify what the emotion means.

In a 1962 experiment, participants injected with adrenaline experienced heart palpitations but only reported feeling 'happy' or 'angry' depending on whether a confederate in the room acted that way. The brain searches for a situational cause for the physical sensation.

You don't know why you feel the way you do.
