You believe you understand how a simple zipper works.

A 2002 study showed that people consistently overestimate their grasp of complex systems until they are forced to explain them.

Participants were asked to describe how items like toilets or zippers function. Their self-assessed knowledge scores plummeted once they attempted to write out the actual mechanical steps. This illusion of depth shields us from the cognitive load of everyday reality.

You confuse familiarity with a mechanism for true understanding.
