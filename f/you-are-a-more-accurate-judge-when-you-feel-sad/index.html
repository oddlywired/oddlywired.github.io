You are a more accurate judge when you feel sad.

In a 2009 study, participants in a negative mood were significantly less susceptible to the misinformation effect than those in a positive mood.

Positive affect signals that the environment is safe, leading to reliance on heuristics and existing mental shortcuts. Sadness triggers a more vigilant, systematic style of processing that pays closer attention to peripheral information. The discomfort you feel in a negative mood forces you to double-check the accuracy of what you are seeing.

Your optimism might be making you less observant.

Where this comes from

Study
Forgas, 2009, The influence of affect on cognition
What the check found
Joseph Forgas's 2009 study 'Can negative affect eliminate the power of suggestion?' confirms that negative mood increases eyewitness accuracy and reduces the misinformation effect.
