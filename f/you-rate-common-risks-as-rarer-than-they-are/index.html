decision making

You rate common risks as rarer than they are.

The availability heuristic leads you to judge probability based on memory, not data.

You estimate the frequency of an event by how easily examples come to mind. If the news covers a dramatic event, you remember it vividly and assume it is more common than boring, high-probability risks. This causes you to misjudge real-world dangers.

Your fears are shaped by your memory, not facts.

Where this comes from

Study
Tversky and Kahneman, 1973
What the check found
The 1973 paper 'Availability: A heuristic for judging frequency and probability' by Tversky and Kahneman is a seminal text that formally established this concept.
