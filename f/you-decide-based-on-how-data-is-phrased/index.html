framing

You decide based on how data is phrased.

In their 1981 paper, Tversky and Kahneman demonstrated that the framing of outcomes as gains or losses influences preferences using a scenario involving 600 potential lives at risk.

The framing effect does not dictate individual choices with certainty; it demonstrates a systematic shift in preference. The 90%/10% survival/mortality example is not the original framing used by Tversky and Kahneman in 1981.

You are not choosing the outcome; you are choosing the description.
