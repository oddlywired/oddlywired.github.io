Your memory of an event is not a summary.

A 1993 study proposed that people disproportionately weight peak intensity and the final moment when evaluating experiences, though research indicates duration remains a significant factor in overall evaluation.

Instead of ignoring the overall duration, the peak-end rule describes a heuristic that summarizes experiences through salient moments while still incorporating duration-based information.

You judge your own life by its highlights rather than its total sum.

Where this comes from

Study
Kahneman, Fredrickson, et al., 1993, Peak-End Rule
What the check found
While the peak-end rule is a valid finding, the claim implies total duration neglect, which modern research clarifies is an efficient heuristic rather than a complete dismissal of duration.

Solid, with limits
