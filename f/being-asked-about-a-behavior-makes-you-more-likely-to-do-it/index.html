Being asked about a behavior makes you more likely to do it.

Asking someone about their intention to perform a future behavior can influence the likelihood of that behavior, but the outcome depends on context and frequency.

While a single instance of questioning intent often increases the likelihood of an action, repeated questioning can lead to a 'polarization effect' that decreases the likelihood of the behavior.

Your own intentions can be manipulated by a simple question.

Where this comes from

Study
Morwitz, Johnson, and Schmittlein, 1993
What the check found
The definition is incomplete because it ignores the conditions under which the effect reverses (e.g., repeated measurements).
Confidence
Solid, with limits
