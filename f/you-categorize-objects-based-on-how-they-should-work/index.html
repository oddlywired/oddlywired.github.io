typicality effect

You categorize objects based on how they 'should' work.

The 'typicality effect' shows that people process category members differently based on how well they fit a prototype.

In categorization tests, participants identify a 'robin' as a bird faster than they identify a 'penguin' as a bird. The brain doesn't just store category labels; it stores a mental average that makes unusual members harder to access.

Your brain prefers the common over the accurate.

Where this comes from

Study
Rosch, 1973, Cognitive Psychology
What the check found
Rosch (1973) is the seminal source for this effect. While the theoretical cause (prototype vs. exemplar storage) is debated, the claim accurately describes the established psychological phenomenon.
