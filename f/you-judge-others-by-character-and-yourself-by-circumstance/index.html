You judge others by character and yourself by circumstance.

The actor-observer asymmetry explains why you interpret your own bad behavior as a situational necessity while labeling identical actions in others as personality flaws.

The fundamental attribution error specifically refers to the observer's tendency to prioritize dispositional explanations for the behavior of others over situational ones.

Your frustration with strangers often comes from lacking their backstory.

Where this comes from

Study
Jones and Harris, 1967
What the check found
While the 1967 study is the canonical demonstration of the bias, the phenomenon described—a disparity between self-attribution and other-attribution—is technically the Actor-Observer Asymmetry.
