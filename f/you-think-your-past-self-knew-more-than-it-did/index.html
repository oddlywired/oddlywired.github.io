You think your past self knew more than it did.

A 1975 study by Baruch Fischhoff demonstrated that knowing an outcome significantly impairs the ability to accurately recall one's initial level of uncertainty.

The study highlights 'hindsight bias,' where individuals misremember their past predictions as being more accurate than they were, rather than suggesting a total inability to reconstruct previous uncertainty.

You cannot look back at your past decisions with the clarity you currently have.

Where this comes from

Study
Baruch Fischhoff, 1975
What the check found
The 1975 paper 'Hindsight ≠ foresight' by Baruch Fischhoff is the seminal work on this topic, but the claim uses absolute language ('unable') to describe a cognitive bias that is probabilistic and effect-based.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
