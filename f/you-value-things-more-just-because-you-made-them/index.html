You value things more just because you made them.

People rate items they partially assembled as equal in value to items built by professionals.

Labor creates a sense of personal investment that overrides objective quality. When you put effort into something, your brain equates that time and energy with quality. You aren't valuing the product; you are valuing the piece of yourself you put into it.

Your pride in your work makes you a poor judge of its market worth.

Where this comes from

Study
Norton et al., 2011, Journal of Consumer Psychology
What the check found
The study (Norton, Mochon, & Ariely) was published online in 2011 and in print in 2012. It confirms that people equate the value of their self-assembled goods to those built by others.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
