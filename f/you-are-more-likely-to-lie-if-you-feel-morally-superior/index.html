You are more likely to lie if you feel morally superior.

The moral licensing effect shows that people who perform a virtuous act often feel entitled to commit a less ethical one afterward.

After completing a prosocial task, participants were more likely to express prejudice or act selfishly. The brain tracks 'moral credits,' allowing you to justify poor behavior because your record is seen as balanced.

Your past good deeds can become a permission slip for current bad habits.

Where this comes from

Study
Monin and Miller (2001)
