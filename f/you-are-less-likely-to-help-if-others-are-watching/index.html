You are less likely to help if others are watching.

The presence of other witnesses significantly reduces the individual probability that someone will offer help in an emergency.

This is due to diffusion of responsibility, where you assume someone else will intervene. You look for cues from others before acting, and when everyone stays still, you conclude that action is unnecessary. Your hesitation is a shared social signal.

A crowd is often the greatest obstacle to taking action.

Where this comes from

Study
Latane and Darley, 1968
What the check found
This is a foundational concept in social psychology. While the effect is context-dependent, the claim correctly reflects the widely established findings of the 1968 Latané and Darley experiments.
