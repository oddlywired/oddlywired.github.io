control

You overestimate your control over random outcomes.

When participants in a 1975 study were asked to pick a card, they were more confident of winning if they picked it themselves.

The illusion of agency makes you feel that your involvement dictates the outcome of chance events. This is why you feel more luck when you roll dice yourself than when someone else rolls them for you.

Your brain confuses participation with causation.

Where this comes from
