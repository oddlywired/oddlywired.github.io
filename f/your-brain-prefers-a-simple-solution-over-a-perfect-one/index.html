satisficing

Your brain prefers a simple solution over a perfect one.

In 1956, Herbert Simon identified that people search for an option that meets a threshold rather than the mathematical maximum.

Rather than evaluating every possible choice to find the absolute best, most people stop the search as soon as they encounter a 'good enough' alternative. This strategy saves precious cognitive resources but often results in overlooking potentially superior outcomes.

You reach satisfaction before you reach excellence.

Where this comes from

Study
Simon, 1956, Psychological Review
What the check found
