Your brain hallucinates words you did not hear.

In the phonemic restoration effect, listeners report hearing a missing phoneme in a word if the gap is replaced by a non-speech sound.

If you hear the word 'legislature' with the 's' replaced by a cough, your brain automatically reconstructs the sound based on context. This occurs in milliseconds without your awareness.

You hear what the context demands, not what is spoken.

Where this comes from

Study
Richard M. Warren, 'Perceptual restoration of missing speech sounds', 1970
