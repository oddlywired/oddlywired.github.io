social dynamics

Your brain registers social rejection as physical pain.

A 2003 study found that the same neural regions that process physical pain light up when you experience social exclusion.

When participants played a virtual ball-tossing game and were suddenly excluded by the other players, their anterior cingulate cortex showed increased activity. Your evolutionary history treats being cast out of the group as a threat to survival. You are wired to find social rejection literal rather than metaphorical.

You do not just feel lonely; you feel injured.

Where this comes from

Study
Eisenberger, Lieberman, & Williams, 2003, Science
What the check found
The study successfully demonstrated ACC activation during exclusion, though the interpretation of this as synonymous with 'physical pain' remains a subject of ongoing debate in neuroscience.
