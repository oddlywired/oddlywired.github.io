One new purchase triggers a cycle of constant upgrades.

The Diderot effect describes how a single acquisition often leads to a spiral of consumption.

When you introduce an item that does not fit your existing collection, you feel the need to replace or update your surrounding possessions to match the new standard.

You are rarely buying the item itself, but the consistency of your environment.
