You judge people by their actions but yourself by your intentions.

Although Jones and Nisbett (1971) proposed that people attribute their own actions to situational factors while attributing others' to dispositional factors, subsequent research, including a 2006 meta-analysis, suggests this asymmetry is not as consistent or widespread as once thought.

The claim overstates the consistency of the actor-observer asymmetry as an established psychological law.

You are the protagonist of your own story, but a background character in everyone else's.
