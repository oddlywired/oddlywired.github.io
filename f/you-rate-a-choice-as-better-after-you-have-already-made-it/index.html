decision making

You rate a choice as better after you have already made it.

A 1956 study demonstrated that participants immediately increased their preference for a chosen item and decreased it for a rejected one.

Post-decision dissonance reduction acts as an internal rationalization tool. You cannot change your choice once made, so you change your attitude to maintain consistency. You convince yourself you made the perfect decision to resolve the discomfort of uncertainty.

You manufacture your own preference to avoid the regret of doubt.
