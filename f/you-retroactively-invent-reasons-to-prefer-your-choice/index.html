decision making

You retroactively invent reasons to prefer your choice.

A 2007 study found that people misremember the features of rejected options to make their chosen one seem superior.

Once you commit to a decision, your memory shifts to highlight the flaws in what you skipped and the benefits of what you kept. Your brain rewrites the history of the decision process.

You are a stranger to the logic you used to choose.

Where this comes from

Study
Henkel and Mather, 2007, Choice-Supportive Bias
What the check found
The study demonstrates that people's memories of features can be altered by their beliefs about which option they originally chose, confirming the core of the claim.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
