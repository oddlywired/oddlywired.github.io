framing effect

Your choice changes based on how the risk is framed.

The framing effect, as demonstrated by Tversky and Kahneman (1981), shows that people react to choices differently depending on whether they are presented as gains or losses.

The '90% survival rate vs. 10% mortality rate' surgery example is more accurately attributed to McNeil et al. (1982), while Tversky and Kahneman's 1981 paper focused on the 'Asian Disease' scenario.

You are not choosing the option; you are choosing the narrative.
