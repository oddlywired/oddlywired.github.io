base rate

You ignore the numbers when a story is better.

A 1973 study showed that people ignore objective probability when presented with a specific, compelling description.

Your brain is wired for narratives, not statistics. A single vivid example overrides a hundred pages of data in your decision-making. You prioritize the plot over the base rate.

A good story is your enemy's best weapon.

Where this comes from

Study
Kahneman and Tversky, 1973, Base Rate Fallacy
What the check found
The 1973 Kahneman and Tversky paper correctly identifies the phenomenon of prioritizing representativeness over base rates, a concept foundational to the study of cognitive biases.
