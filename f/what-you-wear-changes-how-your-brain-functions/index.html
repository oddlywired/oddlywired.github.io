What you wear changes how your brain functions.

Wearing a garment identified as a doctor's coat reduced the number of errors made on specific selective attention tasks by approximately 50% compared to a control group.

The study measured error rates, not a direct doubling of attention span or detail-oriented capability.

You are not just presenting an image; you are priming your own mind.

Where this comes from

Study
Adam & Galinsky, 2012
What the check found
The study found that participants wearing a doctor's coat made roughly half as many errors as the control group; describing this as 'doubling attention to detail' is an imprecise interpretation of statistical results.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
