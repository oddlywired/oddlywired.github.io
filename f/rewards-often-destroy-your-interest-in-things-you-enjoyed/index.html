motivation

Rewards often destroy your interest in things you enjoyed.

The overjustification effect demonstrates that when you are incentivized to perform a task you already like, your intrinsic motivation for that task decreases.

When a task is associated with an external reward, your brain reclassifies the activity as 'work' instead of 'play.' Once the reward is removed, your desire to perform the task drops below your original baseline.

Adding external pressure to a passion can extinguish it.

Where this comes from

Study
Lepper, Greene, and Nisbett (1973)
What the check found
The claim accurately captures the seminal 1973 finding, though it omits that this effect is highly sensitive to reward framing and contingency, rather than occurring every time a reward is introduced.

Well replicated
