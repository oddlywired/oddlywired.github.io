Your brain creates a story to explain your physical reactions.

In a 1962 experiment, subjects were injected with adrenaline and told it was a vitamin, leading them to misinterpret their jittery nerves as happiness or anger depending on the social setting.

When the brain experiences unexplained physiological arousal, it scans the immediate environment for a cue to label the sensation. If a confederate acted euphoric, the subject reported feeling happy. If the confederate acted annoyed, the subject reported feeling angry.

The emotion you feel right now might be a guess based on your surroundings.
