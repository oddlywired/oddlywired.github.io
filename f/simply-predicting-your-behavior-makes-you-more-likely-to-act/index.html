mere measurement effect

Simply predicting your behavior makes you more likely to act.

When people are asked to predict whether they will perform a behavior, they are statistically more likely to perform that behavior later.

This is the mere-measurement effect. The act of thinking through the process of 'doing' creates a mental simulation that reduces the friction to actual execution. It makes the behavior feel more familiar and achievable.

Your intentions are not just thoughts; they are the blueprints for action.
