You prefer a slightly broken robot over a near-perfect one.

Masahiro Mori proposed in 1970 that affinity for humanoid objects drops sharply just before they become indistinguishable from humans.

When a robot looks almost human, small imperfections create a feeling of unease. Your brain struggles to categorize the object, leading to a sense of repulsion. This uncanny valley suggests your social brain expects high-fidelity social cues from anything that resembles a human.

The closer it gets to human, the more sensitive you become to its flaws.
