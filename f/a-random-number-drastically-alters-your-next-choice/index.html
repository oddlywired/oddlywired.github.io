anchoring

A random number drastically alters your next choice.

In a 1974 study, spinning a wheel of fortune influenced participants' subsequent estimates of geographical data.

Even when subjects were told the number was generated randomly, their answers were skewed toward the initial value. The brain uses the first available piece of information as a mental anchor, adjusting final estimates from that starting point regardless of relevance. This anchor exerts gravity on your judgment.

Your first impressions set the boundaries for your later conclusions.
