memory

Your brain reconstructs memories to fit a coherent narrative structure.

Bartlett demonstrated that memory is a reconstructive process influenced by cultural schemas.

Bartlett did not use the term 'essence' to describe memory; he focused on how participants adapted narratives to fit their existing social and cultural frameworks.

Your memory acts more like an author than a camera.

Where this comes from

Study
Bartlett, 1932, Remembering: A Study in Experimental and Social Psychology
What the check found
Bartlett confirmed memory is reconstructive, but the specific framing about storing 'essence' vs. 'raw recordings' is a modern psychological interpretation not found in his 1932 text.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
