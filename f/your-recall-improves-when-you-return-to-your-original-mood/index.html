memory

Your recall improves when you return to your original mood.

A 1981 study showed that participants remembered information better if they were in the same emotional state during retrieval as they were during encoding.

Memory is not just a recording; it is contextually tagged to your physiological state. If you learn something while anxious or happy, that emotional framework acts as a retrieval cue. The brain struggles to bridge the gap between two different states of mind.

You have easier access to your history when you mirror the mood you were in at the time.
