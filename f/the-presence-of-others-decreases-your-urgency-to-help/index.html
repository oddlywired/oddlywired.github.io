social dynamics

The presence of others decreases your urgency to help.

The bystander effect reduces the probability of any single individual offering assistance in an emergency when others are present.

Responsibility is diffused across a crowd, leading individuals to wait for someone else to act first. In a 1968 experiment, students were less likely to help a peer having a seizure if they believed others were also listening.

Social silence is not indifference, but a shared mistake.
