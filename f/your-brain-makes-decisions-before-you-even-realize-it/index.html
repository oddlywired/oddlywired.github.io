Your brain makes decisions before you even realize it.

Libet (1983) observed neural activity preceding conscious awareness by approximately 300 milliseconds, but this interpretation is largely considered outdated.

Modern neuroscience suggests the readiness potential may be stochastic neural noise rather than a precursor to conscious choice, and it is not found in deliberate, complex decision-making scenarios.

Your consciousness is often a spectator to its own intent.

Where this comes from

Study
Libet, 1983, Brain
What the check found
The claim reflects Libet's 1983 finding but ignores decades of subsequent research refuting the idea that these potentials represent a pre-conscious decision-making process.
