If it sounds familiar, you assume it is true.

A 2015 study found that statements were rated as more accurate if they had been heard previously, even when the source was labeled as unreliable.

Known as the illusion of truth effect, this occurs because the brain experiences the mental fluency of recognition as a signal of credibility. We equate the ease of processing with factual accuracy.

Repetition is a highly effective form of persuasion.
