You understand your own car less than you think.

Participants in a 2002 study overestimated their ability to explain how zippers and toilets work.

Researchers asked adults to rate their understanding of everyday devices before and after explaining them step-by-step. The subjects consistently struggled to provide accurate descriptions, revealing a stark gap between perceived and actual knowledge. This discrepancy is known as the illusion of explanatory depth.

We often confuse familiarity with objects for a genuine grasp of their mechanics.
