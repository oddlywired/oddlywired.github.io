People perform better because you expect them to.

Higher performance expectations lead to improved outcomes for the individuals being observed.

In a 1968 study, teachers were told some students had high potential; those students showed greater intellectual growth, not because they were smarter, but because the teachers treated them differently. Your expectations become their reality.

Your view of others is a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Where this comes from

Study
Rosenthal and Jacobson, 1968
What the check found
The Pygmalion Effect is a well-known psychological concept based on the cited study, though the scientific robustness of the original 1968 results is frequently debated due to replication issues.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
