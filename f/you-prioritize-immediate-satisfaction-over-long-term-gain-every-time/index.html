decision making

You prioritize immediate satisfaction over long-term gain every time.

People often choose a smaller, immediate reward over a larger, delayed one, but their preferences frequently reverse in favor of the larger, later reward when both options are set in the future.

The preference is highly dependent on time proximity rather than being a consistent trait.

The brain struggles to value a version of yourself that does not yet exist.

Where this comes from

Study
George Ainslie, 1974
What the check found
Ainslie's work highlights that the preference for immediate rewards is not consistent; it reverses when both options are delayed.
