time perception

Time perception changes as you get older.

Research indicates that your sense of how fast time passes is linked to how much new information you encode; as encoding slows, time feels like it accelerates.

When you are young, your brain processes novel information rapidly, creating a dense network of memories that makes time feel expansive. In adulthood, routines decrease the volume of new experiences, causing the brain to 'compress' these periods in retrospect.

A monotonous life makes years feel like minutes.

Where this comes from

Study
Wittmann, 2015, Felt Time
What the check found
This is a well-established psychological concept known as the 'Holiday Paradox.' While the source is correct and the theory is widely accepted, it describes retrospective time estimation, not prospective timing.
