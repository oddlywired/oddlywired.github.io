reasoning

You ignore information that disagrees with your prior beliefs.

The disconfirmation bias leads you to spend more cognitive effort scrutinizing and attacking evidence that contradicts your existing opinions.

When presented with balanced arguments on a topic, you treat opposing evidence as inherently flawed while accepting supporting evidence with minimal scrutiny. This creates an uneven standard of proof that shields your current worldview from external challenge. The more firmly you hold an opinion, the more aggressively you dismantle conflicting data.

You do not seek truth; you seek validation.

Where this comes from

Study
Lord, Ross, and Lepper, 1979
What the check found
The 1979 study is the seminal paper for this phenomenon, though later psychological literature has refined the nomenclature to distinguish biased assimilation from active disconfirmation.
