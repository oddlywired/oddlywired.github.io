You process sentences faster when they match your physical movement.

The action-sentence compatibility effect demonstrates that manual reaction times are faster when the sentence describes movement in that direction.

Participants responded faster to 'open the drawer' if they had to pull a lever toward themselves. If the sentence was 'close the drawer', their reaction time was faster when pushing the lever away. Your brain simulates the action described in a sentence before you consciously interpret the meaning.

Your mind uses your physical body as a simulator for meaning.
