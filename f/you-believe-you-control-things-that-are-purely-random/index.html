control

You believe you control things that are purely random.

The illusion of control causes you to overestimate your influence over chance-based outcomes.

Whether rolling dice or choosing a lottery ticket, your brain interprets active participation as evidence of agency. The more you are involved in a process, the more you assume you can direct the result.

Participation makes you feel like an architect, even when you are just a spectator.

Where this comes from

Study
Ellen Langer, 1975, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
