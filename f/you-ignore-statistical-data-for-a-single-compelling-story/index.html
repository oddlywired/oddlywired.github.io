You ignore statistical data for a single compelling story.

A 1973 study showed that even when given accurate probabilities, participants prioritized specific personality descriptions over general base rates.

When you encounter a narrative, your brain treats it as more representative than raw data. You prefer the comfort of a vivid individual story over the cold accuracy of population statistics.

An anecdote always outweighs a dataset in your mind.

Where this comes from

Study
Kahneman and Tversky, 1973
What the check found
The 1973 Kahneman and Tversky study (On the Psychology of Prediction) is the seminal work on representativeness. While technically accurate, it is often taught as an absolute flaw rather than a context-sensitive heuristic.
