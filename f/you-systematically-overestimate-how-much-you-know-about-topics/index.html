overconfidence

You systematically overestimate how much you know about topics.

The overconfidence effect is a bias where subjective confidence in judgments often exceeds objective accuracy, though this depends significantly on task difficulty.

It is not a 'consistent' bias; the effect is moderated by the hard-easy effect, as demonstrated in the cited research, where individuals are often underconfident in easy tasks.

Your certainty is often a measurement of your ignorance.

Where this comes from

Study
Lichtenstein, Fischhoff, and Phillips, 1982, Judgment Under Uncertainty
