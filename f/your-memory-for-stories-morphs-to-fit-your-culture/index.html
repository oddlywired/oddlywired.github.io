memory

Your memory for stories morphs to fit your culture.

A 1932 study found that over multiple retellings, recalled stories became shorter, simpler, and more aligned with existing cultural schemas.

Through serial reproduction, the brain systematically omits unfamiliar details and emphasizes elements that match the individual's worldview. Information is not stored as an exact replica but is reconstructed to make sense.

Every time you retell an event, you unconsciously adjust the facts to fit your reality.
