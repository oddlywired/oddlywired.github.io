memory

You believe past events were more predictable than they were.

Hindsight bias causes you to view historical outcomes as inevitable despite the inherent uncertainty of the time.

When people are asked to estimate the probability of an event before it happens, they provide varied answers. Once the outcome is revealed, they revise their memory, claiming they 'knew it all along'.

Your memory reconstructs the past to match the present, erasing your original doubt.
