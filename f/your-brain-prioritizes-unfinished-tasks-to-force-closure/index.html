memory

Your brain prioritizes unfinished tasks to force closure.

The Zeigarnik effect dictates that interrupted tasks are remembered significantly better than completed ones.

Our minds treat uncompleted goals as active mental load, creating a state of internal tension. This pressure persists until we reach completion, which is why your mind keeps circling back to small, undone errands. Once the task is finished, the tension dissipates, and the memory becomes harder to access.

The mental weight of your to-do list is largely the result of unfinished business.
