Anxiety changes what you pay attention to in conversation.

Attentional control theory suggests that anxiety impairs your ability to shift attention away from task-irrelevant stimuli.

When you are anxious, your brain struggles to inhibit distracting information, which reduces your overall processing efficiency. This makes it harder for you to focus on the core meaning of a conversation during high-stress moments.

Anxiety is a cognitive tax on your focus.

Where this comes from

Study
Michael Eysenck, 2007
What the check found
The claim accurately reflects the core tenets of Eysenck et al. (2007), specifically the distinction between the inhibition/shifting functions of the central executive and anxiety's role in processing efficiency.
