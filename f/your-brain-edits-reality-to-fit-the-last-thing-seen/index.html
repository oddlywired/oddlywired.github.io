Your brain edits reality to fit the last thing seen.

The Kuleshov effect demonstrates that viewers project emotions onto a neutral face based on preceding imagery.

In the 1920s, Lev Kuleshov spliced the same expressionless shot of an actor with different clips, such as a bowl of soup or a child in a coffin. Audiences praised the actor's subtle display of hunger and then sorrow, even though the footage was identical. Your mind instinctively bridges these gaps to construct a narrative.

You interpret social cues by linking them to the context you provided.
