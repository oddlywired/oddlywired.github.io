A small request makes you agree to more.

A 1966 study found that homeowners were four times more likely to agree to a large, ugly sign in their yard if they had previously agreed to a small, inconsequential request.

The foot-in-the-door phenomenon happens because you have an internal drive to be consistent. Once you perform a small act of compliance, you shift your self-perception to match your actions, making larger requests harder to refuse.

Your self-image is being manipulated by your own past behavior.

Where this comes from

Study
Freedman and Fraser, 1966, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
