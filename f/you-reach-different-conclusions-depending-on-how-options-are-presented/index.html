decision making

You reach different conclusions depending on how options are presented.

The framing effect describes how your decision-making shifts based on whether an outcome is described in terms of gains or losses.

When participants in a 1981 study were faced with a public health dilemma, they overwhelmingly chose the safer option when it was framed as 'saving lives,' but switched to the riskier option when it was framed as 'lives lost.'

You are not choosing the outcome, but the way someone else described it to you.
