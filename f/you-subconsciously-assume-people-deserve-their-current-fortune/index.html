morality

You subconsciously assume people deserve their current fortune.

The just-world hypothesis leads you to conclude that existing social hierarchies and personal outcomes are the result of merit.

To maintain a sense of order and safety, the mind clings to the idea that actions have proportionate consequences. When you see someone suffer, you may look for flaws in their behavior to validate that the world remains a fair and predictable place. This protects you from the terrifying reality of randomness.

Believing in justice is often just a tool for emotional stability.
