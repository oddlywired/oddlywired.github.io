decision making

You feel more confident in a decision after it is made.

Choice-supportive bias drives the brain to emphasize the positives of a chosen option and ignore its drawbacks post-selection.

Once a choice is finalized, memories are altered to favor that selection. A 2008 study found that participants misremembered the details of an option to make their choice appear superior to the one they rejected. We rewrite our preferences to match our past actions.

You are not choosing based on merit; you are justifying your choice.
