memory

Your brain ignores details that complicate your narrative.

The 'Schema-Consistent Bias' causes you to recall information that fits your existing framework while forgetting incongruent facts.

When a new experience conflicts with your worldview, your brain works to reinterpret or discard the data. This keeps your mental models consistent at the expense of accuracy.

You prefer a coherent story to the messy truth.

Where this comes from

Study
Brewer and Treyens, 'Role of schemata in memory', 1981
What the check found
The cited study (Brewer & Treyens, 1981) is real and accurately demonstrates that schema-consistent items are better recalled than inconsistent ones, though the specific term 'Schema-Consistent Bias' is informal.
