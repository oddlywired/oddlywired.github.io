cognition

Your desire for closure changes the way you process information.

A 1993 study found that individuals with a higher desire for closure form opinions faster and are more resistant to changing them when presented with new evidence.

High-need-for-closure individuals prefer clear, definitive answers over ambiguity. This leads to 'seizing' on the first information provided and 'freezing' their judgment to prevent further mental effort. This cognitive preference dictates how you evaluate new situations daily.

The urge to resolve uncertainty is often stronger than the desire for accuracy.
