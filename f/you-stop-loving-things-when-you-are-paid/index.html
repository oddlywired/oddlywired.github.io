You stop loving things when you are paid.

Introducing external rewards for an activity you enjoy can reduce your intrinsic motivation to do it.

Researchers found that children who were promised a reward for drawing spent less time drawing later compared to those who received no reward. Once you label an activity as work in exchange for payment, your brain shifts from internal curiosity to external calculation. You may lose the drive that made the task enjoyable.

Sometimes incentives undermine your own sense of purpose.
