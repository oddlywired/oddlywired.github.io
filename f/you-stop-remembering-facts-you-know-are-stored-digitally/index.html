memory

You stop remembering facts you know are stored digitally.

The Google effect shows that you are less likely to remember information if you believe it is easily accessible online.

Participants were faster to recall where information was saved on a computer than to recall the information itself. Your brain offloads the effort of retention when it trusts that the data is retrievable.

You have traded internal knowledge for the ability to locate it.
