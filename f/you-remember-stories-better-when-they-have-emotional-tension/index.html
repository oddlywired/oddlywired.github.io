memory

You remember stories better when they have emotional tension.

The 'arousal-biased competition' model states that emotional stimuli occupy your memory resources at the expense of neutral background details.

During an emotionally charged event, the brain prioritizes the central source of the arousal. This makes the emotional core of a memory vivid, while the surrounding context often becomes completely lost to you.

You remember the heat, but not the room.

Where this comes from

Study
Mather and Sutherland, 2011, Perspectives on Psychological Science
What the check found
The ABC model as proposed by Mather and Sutherland (2011) accurately describes the trade-off where arousal-biased priority suppresses competition from neutral distractors.
