Asking for your plan makes you likely to follow it.

The mere measurement effect describes how measuring intentions can influence behavior, though the outcome depends on the frequency of questioning.

While initial measurement can increase behavior, research shows that repeated questioning can lead to a decrease in the probability of that behavior.

Your plans are more likely to materialize once you verbalize them.

Where this comes from

Study
Morwitz and Fitzsimons, 2004, The Mere Measurement Effect
What the check found
The claim omits the caveat that repeated measurement reduces the effect. Additionally, the foundational research is typically attributed to Morwitz et al. (1993), not a 2004 paper by Morwitz and Fitzsimons.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
