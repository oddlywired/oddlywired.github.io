negativity dominance

You confuse your focus with reality.

The 'negativity dominance' principle shows that people give disproportionate weight to singular negative features in a multi-attribute evaluation.

When evaluating an object or person, a single 'bad' trait—like a bad smell or a rude comment—will override multiple 'good' traits. The brain is wired to identify threats first, and it struggles to balance a negative signal against a positive sum.

You are built to look for the one thing missing.

Where this comes from

Study
Rozin and Royzman, 2001, Personality and Social Psychology Review
What the check found
The Rozin and Royzman (2001) paper defines negativity dominance exactly as the claim states, distinguishing it from general negativity bias.
