You lose time with every shift in focus.

A 2001 study found that task-switching costs result in significant drops in efficiency when moving between different types of mental work.

The brain requires time to reorganize its rules for a new task, a process called 'switch cost'. Even if the switch takes less than a second, frequent changes add up to hours of lost productivity over a week. You are not a multitasker; you are a rapid switcher.

Constant context switching is a hidden tax on your time.

Where this comes from

Study
Rubinstein, Meyer, & Evans, 2001, Journal of Experimental Psychology
What the check found
The study (Rubinstein, Meyer, & Evans, 2001) exists and established the existence of 'switch costs,' but the specific translation into 'hours of lost productivity' is a common interpretative extrapolation, not a primary result.
