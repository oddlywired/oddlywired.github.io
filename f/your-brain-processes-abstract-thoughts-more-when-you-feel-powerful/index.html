power

Your brain processes abstract thoughts more when you feel powerful.

Research shows that feeling a sense of power shifts your cognitive processing toward abstract, big-picture thinking.

Participants primed with high power focused more on the gist of a situation rather than minor details. Low-power priming led participants to focus on local, specific components of a task. The power shift changes how you categorize objects and plan for the future.

How much control you feel you have changes the resolution at which you view the world.

Where this comes from

Study
Smith and Trope, 2006, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

The claim accurately reflects the central thesis of the cited 2006 Smith & Trope paper, which is the foundational study for the link between social power and construal level theory.
