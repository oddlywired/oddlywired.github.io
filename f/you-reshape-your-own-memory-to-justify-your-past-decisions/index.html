hindsight

You reshape your own memory to justify your past decisions.

Hindsight bias allows you to believe that past events were more predictable than they actually were at the time.

Once you know the outcome of an event, your brain automatically updates your memory to make the event seem inevitable. You rewrite your personal narrative to align with your current knowledge.

You are not remembering your past; you are editing it to make yourself look wiser.

Where this comes from

Study
Fischhoff, 1975, Journal of Experimental Psychology
