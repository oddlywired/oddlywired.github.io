cognitive bias

Your choice depends entirely on how options are phrased.

In the 1981 study, 72% of participants chose a certain outcome of saving 200 out of 600 lives over a risky option with a 1/3 probability of saving everyone.

The original study utilized a hypothetical scenario involving 600 lives at risk, not percentage-based treatment survival rates.

You are making choices based on the words provided, not the facts involved.

Where this comes from

Study
Tversky and Kahneman, 1981, Science
What the check found
The 72% figure is accurate to the positive-frame arm of the study, but the description of the treatment is factually incorrect as it replaces absolute counts with percentage probabilities.
