Your brain creates a false sense of visual continuity.

The 'flicker paradigm' reveals that people often fail to notice massive changes in a scene if they occur during a brief flicker.

The brain does not store a detailed, frame-by-frame image of your environment. Instead, it maintains a skeletal gist and fills in the rest with assumptions based on past experience. When the scene flickers, your brain's memory of the 'gist' overrides the actual visual update, masking the change.

What you see is a reconstruction, not a recording.
