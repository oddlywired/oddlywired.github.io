availability heuristic

You overestimate risks that appear frequently in the news.

People judge the probability of an event by how easily they can recall similar instances from memory.

This is the availability heuristic. Because news outlets report rare, dramatic events more often than common, dull ones, these events are more 'available' in your mind. You equate this mental ease with actual frequency, leading to irrational fear of unlikely outcomes.

Your fear is a reflection of your media exposure, not reality.

Where this comes from

Tversky and Kahneman, 1973, Cognitive Psychology
