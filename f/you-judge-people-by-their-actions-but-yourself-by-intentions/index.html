You judge people by their actions but yourself by intentions.

The hypothesis of a foundational actor-observer asymmetry in attribution, while influential in early social psychology, has been largely refuted by meta-analytic evidence.

Empirical testing has failed to consistently demonstrate the actor-observer asymmetry across the contexts where it was expected to appear.

You judge everyone else by their output, but yourself by your struggle.

Where this comes from

Study
Jones & Nisbett, 1971, Attribution: Perceiving the Causes of Behavior
What the check found
While Jones and Nisbett proposed the hypothesis, subsequent meta-analyses failed to find evidence that this asymmetry is a universal or foundational feature of social cognition.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
