You unconsciously associate vertical position with social status.

Participants in a 2005 study identified words as 'high' or 'low' status based on their vertical screen position.

The brain maps status onto vertical space. People identify high-status groups significantly faster when they are displayed at the top of a screen. We automatically link power to elevation, even when the task has no actual social context.

You possess a built-in mental shortcut that views vertical height as a proxy for authority.

Where this comes from

Study
Schubert, 2005, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
Schubert (2005) is a seminal paper in social cognition demonstrating that participants associate vertical space with power, facilitating faster categorization when power-related words align with 'top' positions.
