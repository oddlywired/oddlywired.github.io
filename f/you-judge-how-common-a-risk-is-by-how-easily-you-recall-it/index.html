You judge how common a risk is by how easily you recall it.

The availability heuristic is a mental shortcut that relies on immediate examples that come to mind when evaluating a topic or decision.

If you can think of a vivid example of something—like a plane crash or a shark attack—you overestimate the probability of that event happening. The brain treats ease of recall as a proxy for frequency. If it is easy to imagine, your brain treats it as a high-probability event.

You mistake the vividness of a memory for the frequency of the event.

Where this comes from

Study
Tversky & Kahneman, 1973, Cognitive Psychology
