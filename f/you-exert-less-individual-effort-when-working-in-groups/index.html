You exert less individual effort when working in groups.

The phenomenon of reduced individual output in groups is known as the Ringelmann effect, which was later named social loafing in 1979.

Ringelmann conducted his research in the 1880s; the term 'social loafing' was coined by Latané, Williams, and Harkins in 1979.

The collective output often masks individual decline.
