moral licensing

Doing a good deed makes us more likely to cheat later.

A 2010 study found that participants who bought green products were more likely to lie and steal in a subsequent task.

Researchers compared participants who shopped in an eco-friendly store with those who shopped in a conventional store. In a later, unrelated computer task, the eco-friendly shoppers were significantly more likely to cheat to earn extra money.

Your brain uses recent good behavior as a permission slip to slack off.

Where this comes from

Study
Mazar & Zhong, 2010, Psychological Science
What the check found
The 2010 study by Mazar and Zhong in Psychological Science indeed found that participants who bought green products in a lab store subsequently cheated and stole more in an unrelated task (the moral licensing effect).
Confidence
Well replicated
